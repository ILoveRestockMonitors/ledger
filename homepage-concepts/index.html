Five new homepages for Ledger

Five directions for the Home screen, each designed from scratch. Each one follows a different scroll-craft page style, so scrolling does something different on every one. All of them borrow the simplest ideas from Dollarwise:

  • Needs, wants and savings (50/30/20)
  • One clear "left to spend" number
  • A short weekly check-in
  • Sorted for you, easy to change

Every number is real math on the app's sample data (Alex and Juniper Studio, September 24, 2026). Buttons work, but nothing is saved. Open each one and scroll slowly.

1

Console

The home screen is the working app. Scroll down and the next 30 days play forward: bills and paydays land, and your cash balance moves with them.

Feels like
A calm, dense control panel. Everything in one place.
Scrolling
Moves a date cursor through the next month. You can also drag it.
Ends with
A one-line box to add a transaction. Try "Juniper Kitchen 12.50".
Colors, type
 Geist
Open Console
2

The Seam

Personal and Business side by side, the whole way down. The line between them is the Everything / Personal / Business switch: drag it to one side and that side takes the page.

Feels like
Two lives, one view. Easy to compare month to month.
Scrolling
Each row compares the two sides. At the end, the line slides over to whichever side needs you this week.
Ends with
The charges waiting for an answer, in the side that won.
Colors, type
 Bricolage Grotesque, Figtree
Open The Seam
3

The Almanac

Your month, written as a short letter in four chapters. Every underlined dollar amount opens its own receipt in the margin, so you can see exactly what adds up to it.

Feels like
Reading, not operating. Quiet and reassuring.
Scrolling
Turns chapters: where it went, what's coming, what you're building, the week in review.
Ends with
A one-line invitation to finish the weekly review.
Colors, type
 Newsreader, Instrument Sans
Open The Almanac
4

The Poster

One number you need to know, as big as the screen. Then the words NEEDS, WANTS and SAVINGS gain weight as you scroll through September, so the type itself shows how much of each you've used.

Feels like
Bold and simple. The closest to Dollarwise's "just tell me" spirit.
Scrolling
Squeezes the big number, then replays the month in the weight of the letters.
Ends with
The page flips to cream and goes quiet: add a transaction, review this week.
Colors, type
 Anybody, Schibsted Grotesk
Open The Poster
5

Pulse

Your week as fast, colorful screens, one for each thing that happened. Tap Need, Want or Save on each. The gauge in the top bar drains and refills as the week plays back.

Feels like
Quick and fun, like a story recap. Built for a weekly habit.
Scrolling
Hard cuts between colors. At the high point the top bar opens up to show what's left.
Ends with
A giant Check in button that takes you to anything still unanswered.
Colors, type
 Unbounded, Onest
Open Pulse

Picking one

Reply with the number you like best. You can also mix parts, for example "Console, but with the Almanac's receipts" or "Pulse as the weekly check-in inside Console".

Once you approve a direction, it gets the full treatment: Transactions, Budgets, Goals, Reports, Settings and the phone layout, plus a matching light and dark mode.
